About SmartLoanCalc

An independent educational mortgage website. Here is who we are, how we work and how we are funded.

What SmartLoanCalc is

SmartLoanCalc is an independent educational website that helps people understand the cost of a home loan. The centerpiece is a free mortgage payment calculator that estimates principal and interest, property tax, homeowners insurance, mortgage insurance and homeowners association dues, and shows how a loan is paid down over time. Alongside the calculator we publish plain-language guides about mortgages and home buying, an extra-payment calculator, and pages that explain exactly how our math works.

We are not a lender, a mortgage broker or a financial adviser. We do not take loan applications, we do not sell your information to lenders and we are not paid when you choose a particular loan. Our goal is simple: help you see the real numbers before you make one of the largest financial decisions of your life.

Why we built it

Mortgage numbers can be confusing. A listing may show a payment that leaves out property tax, insurance, mortgage insurance and association dues. A lender’s quote may bundle fees into the rate. Many calculators give an answer without explaining where it came from. We wanted a tool that is easy to use, shows the full monthly cost, and explains its method so you can check it yourself. Everything on the site is written to support that purpose.

What you will find here

  • The mortgage calculator. Estimate your monthly payment with taxes, insurance, PMI and HOA dues, with a payment breakdown chart and a yearly amortization schedule.
  • The extra payment calculator. See how much sooner you could pay off a loan, and how much interest you could save, by paying extra principal.
  • 15 in-depth guides. Topics include using a calculator accurately, 15-year versus 30-year loans, PMI, closing costs, affordability, fixed versus adjustable rates, amortization, refinancing, bidding wars, extra payments, escrow, inspections, credit scores, closing day and first-time buyer mistakes.
  • How It Works and FAQ pages. The formulas, test cases and answers to common questions.

Our editorial standards

Because mortgage decisions involve real money, we hold our content to a few simple standards.

  • Check the math. Every worked example is calculated with the same formula the calculator uses, and tables are generated from that formula rather than typed by hand.
  • Say what is an example. When we use hypothetical figures, such as a made-up rate tier, we label them as illustrative. We do not present invented stories as real experiences.
  • Use reliable sources. Where we state rules, such as the Loan Estimate timeline or PMI cancellation thresholds, we point to official sources like the Consumer Financial Protection Bureau (CFPB) and the Department of Housing and Urban Development (HUD).
  • Avoid absolutes. We explain trade-offs and note when the right choice depends on your situation. We do not tell you what you must do.
  • Show when we reviewed it. Each guide shows when it was last reviewed, and we update guides when rules or best practices change.

How we research and write

Each guide starts with a question a home buyer or owner might actually ask, such as how much extra payments save or what escrow does. We identify the standard formulas, rules and typical ranges that apply, check them against official consumer resources, and build worked examples with the calculator’s own math. We then write the explanation in plain language, add a checklist where it helps, and link to related guides so you can follow a topic in depth. Rates, taxes and rules change, so we describe typical ranges and tell you to confirm current figures with your lender.

Accuracy and corrections

We make mistakes like anyone, and we want them fixed quickly. If you find an error in the calculator or a guide, email us with the page, the inputs you used and what you expected. We will check it, correct the page if we find a problem and update the review date. We do not quietly leave known errors in place. If a correction changes an important number, we will say so on the page.

Advertising does not influence our calculations, our guides or which loan options we describe. We do not accept payment to recommend a lender, and we do not publish sponsored content disguised as advice. If that ever changes, we will label it clearly.

Your privacy

The numbers you enter in the calculators are processed in your browser and are not sent to us. We use standard analytics and advertising technology that rely on cookies, which we describe in our Privacy Policy, along with the choices you have. We never ask for your Social Security number, bank account details or credit card number.

What we are not

  • We are not a lender, broker or adviser, and nothing here is financial, legal or tax advice.
  • We cannot approve you for a loan or tell you which rate you will receive.
  • We do not guarantee that estimates match your lender’s figures. Real quotes depend on your credit, down payment, location and loan type.
  • We do not replace professionals such as loan officers, attorneys, tax advisers and HUD-approved housing counselors, and we encourage you to talk to them.

Who the site is for

SmartLoanCalc is built for first-time buyers who want to understand what a payment includes, for current owners who are thinking about extra payments or refinancing, and for anyone comparing loan terms. You do not need to know mortgage vocabulary to use it. Each page explains terms as they come up, and the FAQ covers common questions in short answers.

Trust is earned by being clear. That is why we explain our formulas, label hypothetical numbers, show review dates and tell you who is responsible for the site. Explaining or fixing the difference is one of the most useful things we can do, and it helps every reader who comes after you.
