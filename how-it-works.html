How It Works: The Real Math Behind Your Mortgage

No jargon. No hidden agendas. Just the straight facts about how we calculate your payment, and why the banks hope you never understand it.

Published by the SmartLoanCalc Founder | Updated November 2026

This page explains exactly how SmartLoanCalc calculates your numbers, so you can check them yourself.

The core formula

Fixed-rate mortgages use the standard amortization formula: M = P · r(1+r)ⁿ / ((1+r)ⁿ − 1).

  • M: monthly principal-and-interest payment
  • P: loan amount (home price minus down payment)
  • r: monthly rate (annual rate ÷ 12, e.g. 6.5% → 0.005417)
  • n: number of payments (years × 12)

The inputs

  • Down payment: below 20%, lenders usually require PMI on conventional loans. PMI can generally be requested to end at 80% loan-to-value and ends automatically at 78% of the original value if you are current on payments.
  • Interest rate: on $400,000 over 30 years, 6.0% gives about $2,398 per month and 7.0% about $2,661, a difference of roughly $263 a month, or about $94,700 over the loan.
  • Loan term: with the same rate, a 15-year loan has a higher payment and far less total interest than a 30-year loan. Note that 15-year loans usually carry a lower rate than 30-year loans, which widens the gap further.
  • Property tax, insurance, HOA: enter your own estimates. Tax varies widely by county and insurance by location and home.

Amortization example

For $400,000 at 6.5% over 30 years, the payment is about $2,528. Month 1: $2,167 interest, $361 principal. Around year 15: about $1,630 interest and $900 principal. Interest is charged on the remaining balance, so early payments are interest-heavy.

Extra payments

Extra principal reduces the balance that interest is charged on. On a $300,000, 30-year loan at 6.5%, an extra $100 a month shortens the loan by roughly four years and saves about $60,000 in interest. Whether to prepay or invest or pay off higher-rate debt depends on your situation. Confirm your loan has no prepayment penalty.

Limits of the estimate

Rates, taxes, insurance, PMI and lender fees differ by borrower. Use this as a planning tool and rely on your lender's Loan Estimate for actual numbers. See our Terms and About page.
