Mortgage FAQ

Short, sourced answers to common home-financing questions.

Before you read

These answers are general and educational. Rates, rules and fees change and differ by lender and location, so confirm important details with your lender or an official source such as the CFPB. If your question is not covered, send it to us and we will consider adding an answer.

How the calculator works

How accurate is SmartLoanCalc?

It uses the standard fixed-rate amortization formula, which is the same math lenders use for principal and interest. Your lender’s figures can still differ because of rate, fees, taxes, insurance, mortgage insurance and rounding. Treat our results as planning estimates and compare them with your Loan Estimate. The full method is explained on our How It Works page.

Does the calculator store my numbers?

No. Calculations run in your browser and the numbers you enter are not sent to us. Like most websites, we may use analytics and advertising cookies, described in our Privacy Policy.

Why is my lender’s payment different?

Common reasons are a different interest rate, mortgage insurance, escrow for taxes and insurance, discount points, a different loan amount after fees or a different payment date. Ask your lender for an itemized breakdown and compare it line by line.

Does it include taxes, insurance and HOA dues?

Yes. Click “Add Taxes, Insurance & HOA” and enter yearly tax and insurance and monthly dues. Look up the actual tax bill and get an insurance quote for the best accuracy.

How is PMI estimated?

When your down payment is under 20%, we add 0.5% of the loan per year as a rough estimate. Real PMI depends on credit score, down payment and loan type, so replace it with a lender quote.

Down payments and mortgage insurance

Do I need 20% down?

No. Many loans allow less. Conventional loans start from about 3% down for some borrowers and FHA loans from 3.5%. With less than 20% down you generally pay PMI or FHA mortgage insurance. Compare the cost with the benefit of buying sooner, and keep an emergency fund after closing.

How do I get rid of PMI?

On a conventional loan you can generally request cancellation when the balance reaches 80% of the original home value, if you are current and meet your lender’s requirements. It ends automatically at 78% on schedule. FHA insurance follows different rules. Our PMI guide explains the details.

Is a bigger down payment always better?

A larger down payment lowers your loan, payment and PMI, but it also uses cash you might need for repairs and emergencies. Many buyers prefer a balance: enough down to keep the payment comfortable and enough savings left over.

Rates, terms and lenders

How do I compare lenders?

Ask at least three lenders for a Loan Estimate on the same day, with the same loan type and rate lock period. Compare the interest rate, origination charges and total closing costs. Lenders must provide the Loan Estimate within three business days of your application.

Should I choose a 15-year or 30-year loan?

A 15-year loan has a higher payment and much less total interest. A 30-year loan has a lower required payment and more flexibility. See our 15-year vs 30-year comparison, and choose the payment you can carry comfortably.

What credit score do I need?

Conventional loans commonly need about 620, and FHA loans can allow 580 with 3.5% down. Higher scores generally earn better rates. Rules vary by lender and change over time, so ask. See our credit score guide.

What is the difference between fixed and adjustable rates?

A fixed rate stays the same for the entire loan. An adjustable rate is fixed for an introductory period and then changes. Read fixed vs adjustable-rate mortgages before choosing an ARM.

Costs of buying

What are closing costs?

They are fees and prepaid items paid at closing, such as lender charges, appraisal, title insurance and prepaid taxes and insurance. They are commonly estimated at 2% to 5% of the purchase price. See our closing costs guide.

What is escrow, and why can my payment change?

Your servicer may collect part of your yearly property tax and insurance each month and pay the bills for you. If those bills rise, your payment rises even on a fixed-rate loan. Read escrow explained.

What is amortization?

It is the schedule of how each payment splits between interest and principal. Early payments are mostly interest. Open the amortization schedule in the calculator to see your own loan, or read how amortization works.

Do I need a home inspection?

It is not required by most lenders, but it is strongly recommended. An inspection can reveal costly problems before you commit. See why inspections matter.

Paying down and refinancing

Can I pay extra on my mortgage?

Usually yes, but check for a prepayment penalty and make sure extra money is applied to principal. Use the Extra Payment Calculator on our homepage to estimate the time and interest saved, and read our extra payment guide.

When does refinancing make sense?

When the new rate and costs give you a short break-even period and you will keep the loan longer than that. Compare total interest too, not just the payment. See our refinancing guide.

What happens if the appraisal comes in low?

The lender bases the loan on the lower value. You can renegotiate the price, pay the difference in cash, challenge the appraisal or walk away if your contract allows. See our bidding war guide.

Planning your purchase

How much house can I afford?

Lenders compare your monthly debts to your gross income. A common guideline is housing at or below 28% of income and total debt at or below 36%, though many loans allow higher ratios. Choose a payment you can carry comfortably after savings and repairs. Our affordability guide walks through examples.

How much should I keep saved after buying?

Many planners suggest an emergency fund covering several months of expenses, plus money for repairs. A common rule of thumb is to budget 1% to 2% of the home’s value each year for maintenance. Do not spend every dollar on the down payment.

Should I get pre-approved before house hunting?

Yes. Pre-approval involves documents and a credit check, and it shows what a lender may offer. Sellers also take offers more seriously. Treat the approved amount as a ceiling and not as a target, and compare lenders before you commit.

What are the biggest mistakes first-time buyers make?

Common ones include not comparing lenders, budgeting only for the payment, using all savings for the down payment, skipping the inspection and changing finances before closing. See common first-time buyer mistakes for a full list.

What happens at closing?

You review and sign the loan documents, pay your cash to close and receive the keys once funds are disbursed and the deed is recorded. You should get the Closing Disclosure at least three business days before. Read what happens at closing.
